# CAP-019: adjacencies at the end of a detail

*Implements* [FEA-005: inspector views](../features/FEA-005.md). Index entry:
[capabilities/README.md](README.md#CAP-019).

An object's adjacencies are shown only in its detail ([CAP-007: detail view](CAP-007.md)), after its
properties, in its schema's declared order: comments last where a schema
declares them last. Where a chip is expected, only chips are shown: no
adjacency list is nested under a chip, in a browser or in an adjacency
list. Deeper levels are reached by inspecting a linked object: a topic's
detail ends with its threads, and a thread's detail with its replies.

An adjacency is previewed as a single chip per entry, divided into two
columns. The left column shows the entry's properties. The right column
shows a pill `{object key} : {object label}` of the linked object, which
is clicked to inspect it. An object without a label of its own (a
comment) is named by its summary, else its first text property, shown as
prose. Where the object has a creation identity and datestamp ([CAP-020: provenance display](CAP-020.md)),
the pill is headed by `{identity} @ {datestamp}`, in italics and a reduced
font.

Adjacencies that are part of the object's ownership path may be shown
abbreviated in breadcrumb form.

## Decided by an agent, for review

Where this capability was silent, the simplest default consistent with it and with mbse-schemas, each *proposed*
until reviewed, labeled with the repository and number it was decided under.

- **(mbse-react 9)** **The inspector's adjacency lists**: one section per named adjacency of the object's schema, in the
  schema's order. Its `y` is each schema that declares an adjacency to the same relation through another link (several
  where a link is filled by several schemas, as `work.About`'s `topic`): one [CAP-008: adjacency as a list of
  chips](CAP-008.md) request each, their results together. Until they arrive, or where every one is refused, the section
  shows the detail view's own entries, labelled from it. An adjacency with no entry in the detail view is not requested.
- **(mbse-react 28)** **Adjacencies only in a detail** (this capability, as
  revised): an object's adjacency lists are the end of its detail, in its schema's order, each a row of chips
  (mbse-react 33, 36); no list is nested under a chip. A deeper level is a click away: a topic's detail ends with its
  threads, a thread's (inspected) with its replies. (First built as nesting one level deep under each linked chip, which
  the user found confusing: "Showing comments *under* the chip is doubly so.")
- **(mbse-react 33)** **An adjacency's preview is one chip per entry**: two columns, the entry's properties on the left and, on the right, a pill `ID : name` of the
  linked object, a click on which inspects it. Its ID is its path's last key (`Views.lastKey`: its owning entry's key
  values, or a singleton's name). Its name is its own label; where it has none ([CAP-005: label and summary
  view](CAP-005.md)'s fallback), its summary, else its first text property (a comment's text), either shown as prose,
  the pill wrapping to three lines; else its schema's name. Where the entry has no properties, the pill takes the whole
  chip. Where the object's provenance holds its creation ([CAP-020: provenance display](CAP-020.md)), the pill is
  headed, in small italics, by `{identity} @ {datestamp}`; no byline sits outside the chip. The object's other
  properties wait for its detail. (First built as the object's chip beside its entry's, which read as two things for
  one: "why are there two chips but one thingy?"; then with the byline under the chip, "disembodied from the pill".)
- **(mbse-react 34)** **The ownership path is a breadcrumb**: an owned object's inspector header shows its path, read from its reference, as `singleton › key
  › …`: its singleton's name, then each owning entry's key values, itself last; each ancestor is inspected on a click (a
  singleton with its directory schema; a deeper owner, were there one, with no schema known). The ownership adjacency
  (an adjacency marked `owner`) gets no list in the detail. A singleton has no breadcrumb. No subtitle repeats a
  schema's name: the inspected object's schema and each adjacency's relation are tooltips on the label and the section's
  heading, since the breadcrumb and the adjacency's name say as much.
