# CAP-239: grid adjacency

*Implements* [FEA-056: fungible diagramming keeps agents and humans in the loop](../features/FEA-056.md).

A grid container reorders a child when an adjacency spec is added, reverts it to its original insertion position when
the spec is removed, quantizes or rejects a rotation it cannot represent, and gives children sharing a "from" shape and
rotation a nested cell container, in Python and TypeScript.

## Design

As [the diagrams proposal](../../proposals/DIAGRAMS.md) states it, on [CAP-238: container schemas and grid insertion](CAP-238.md)'s grids;
mbse-diagrams' [pull request 3](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-diagrams/pull/3) builds it, `Framework.Container`'s
`adjoined` and `unjoined` in both languages, with suite ADJ.

## Decided by an agent, for review

Where this capability and the proposal were silent, the simplest default consistent with them, with CAP-238's
decisions and with mbse-schemas, each *proposed* until reviewed, labeled with the repository and number it was decided
under.

- **(mbse-diagrams 3)** **Quantized, never rejected.** A grid represents four sides, so every rotation is quantized to
  the nearest quarter turn, clockwise from north, half way between two to the clockwise one (45 degrees is east).
- **(mbse-diagrams 3)** **"A simple change in insertion order"** is one: the child moved to the one position in the
  grid's sequence that puts its cell on the spec's side of the other's, everything after shifting. In a grid that grows
  rows, east is the next position and west the previous, within a row; south is a span on, and north a span back. In
  one that grows columns, south and north are within a column, and east and west a span on and back. Where no position
  does, at the end of a row or a column, before the first or past the last, the spec is ignored, and stays in the
  logical layout.
- **(mbse-diagrams 3)** **A shared cell.** A spec whose side of the "from" shape is a cell already holding a shape a
  spec from it along the same side placed joins that cell, rather than taking one of its own. A cell of several shapes
  is a container of its own: it grows down, `"rows"`, for east and west, and across, `"columns"`, for north and south,
  its shapes in the order their specs were applied, and its extents those of its shapes.
- **(mbse-diagrams 3)** **Reverting.** Once a spec to a child is removed, the child is put back before the first cell
  whose first shape has a later original insertion position, leaving a shared cell, which shrinks back to one; then
  the specs to it that remain are applied, in order.
- **(mbse-diagrams 3)** **By the caller, for now**, as CAP-238's: `adjoined(from_shape, to_shape)` within the
  transaction that adds a spec, `unjoined(to_shape)` within the one that removes it; `layout` applies a container's
  existing specs once its children are placed.

## Open questions

- **One move can break another spec.** Moving a child to meet one spec can move another out of the place an earlier
  spec put it: putting a child west of the first in a row moves the first, and a child reverting out of a shared cell
  moves the cells after it. Restoring every rule after each change, and ignoring the later of two that conflict, is
  [CAP-240: conflicting layout rules ignored and requeued](README.md#CAP-240)'s.
