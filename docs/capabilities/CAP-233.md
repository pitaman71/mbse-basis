# CAP-233: literals of any value

*Implements* [FEA-024: expressions as data](../features/FEA-024.md). Index entry: [capabilities/README.md](README.md#CAP-233). *Relates to* [CAP-095: Basic terms as data](CAP-095.md), [CAP-096: collections in expressions](CAP-096.md), [CAP-071: unrestricted OfAny serializes with 1-level tag](CAP-071.md), [CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md), [CAP-227: the Python3 expression dialect](CAP-227.md), [CAP-228: the TypeScript5 expression dialect](CAP-228.md).

## As built: mbse-expressions

Built in mbse-expressions' stages 2 to 4 of [the complete dialects](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/dialects/README.md#stages-proposed-once-decided)
(main at 8804e1e, unreleased until 0.8.0); the choices made where the decisions were silent are items 38
([the port's API, Settled in the complete dialects' stage 2](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/port/API.md#settled-in-the-complete-dialects-stage-2-for-the-users-review)),
53 ([Settled in stage 3 (Python3)](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/port/API.md#settled-in-stage-3-python3-for-the-users-review))
and 74 ([Settled in stage 4 (TypeScript5)](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/port/API.md#settled-in-stage-4-typescript5-for-the-users-review)). In 0.7.0 a
`literal`'s `value` was an `OfAny` restricted to the five basic scalars ([CAP-095: Basic terms as data](CAP-095.md)).

- **Stage 2, the framework's literal**, `Expressions.Framework.Literal`, is a domain representation
  ([PRI-023: domain representations](../PRINCIPLES.md#PRI-023)): `value`, an unrestricted `Schemas.OfAny`, any value,
  scalars, containers and value objects, written on the wire with the one-level tag of an unrestricted `OfAny`
  ([CAP-071: unrestricted OfAny serializes with 1-level tag](CAP-071.md)); `text`, its source spelling, unvalidated;
  and `problems`, one indicator per error, so that a literal whose text is invalid may have no `value`. Validation
  reports each problem; printers write `text` where it is present, else the value's default spelling; translation
  matches a literal by its value alone; evaluation reads `value`. A literal written in code has a `value` and no
  `text`. A container may be written as a literal or by a constructor (`list`, `mapping`,
  [CAP-096: collections in expressions](CAP-096.md)). Whitespace and comments between tokens are not a literal's.
- **Stage 3, Python3's literal**, `Expressions.Python3.Literal`, holds a basic scalar, refines the framework's
  ([CAP-167: schemas refined by other schemas](CAP-167.md)) and adds what its text encodes: `prefix`, `quote`, `base`
  and `separators`; what else a text spells (an exponent, `0X` against `0x`) is in `text` alone. Python's `FromText`
  fills them from the source. What mbse-schemas has no schema for, `None`, `...` and complex numbers, is not a value
  `OfAny` holds, and is Python3's own `constant` kind
  ([CAP-227: the Python3 expression dialect](CAP-227.md#as-built-mbse-expressions)).
- **Stage 4, TypeScript5's literal**, `Expressions.TypeScript5.Literal`, holds a boolean, a number, a bigint or a
  string, refines the framework's and adds what its text encodes: `quote` (`'` or `"`), `base` (2, 8, 10 or 16) and
  `separators`. A number's value is a float (binary64, as JavaScript's numbers are) and a bigint's an int, so a
  bigint's `n` is the value's type, not an attribute. What mbse-schemas has no schema for, `null` and a regular
  expression (`pattern` and `flags`, each as written), is TypeScript5's own `constant` kind, with `text` and
  `problems`; `undefined` is an `identifier`
  ([CAP-228: the TypeScript5 expression dialect](CAP-228.md#as-built-mbse-expressions)). A literal with no `text` is
  printed in its value's default spelling, a number as JavaScript's `String(number)`.

Not yet built: the other complete dialects' literals, C's `u` and `f` suffixes among them (kept until
[CAP-232: domains inferred for terms](CAP-232.md) gives them meaning), in their own stages.

## Decided

As the user decided: adopted, each the rule as stated, quoted where the wording matters.

- **A literal holds any value** (2026-11-02, [the complete-dialects proposal](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/dialects/README.md#decided),
  Decided). Told that "the five basic scalars" was the agent's gloss of "yes restrict `literal` to scalars"
  (2026-10-30), the user: "I don't think that rule about "literal is restricted to the five basic scalars" is useful.
  May have been a hallucination or exaggeration." To the recommendation (an unrestricted `literal`, and a `Python3`
  constant kind for `None`, `...` and complex numbers): "Yes. This merits its own CAP item too". It supersedes the
  restriction to scalars ([CAP-097: value domains of published standards](CAP-097.md#decided), Decided).
- **A literal is a domain representation** (2026-11-02): asked whether a literal keeps its spelling beside its value,
  the user: "See PRI-023"; to the reading above (`text`, `problems`, `value`): "it does".
