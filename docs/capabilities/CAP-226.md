# CAP-226: complete expression dialects

*Implements* [FEA-024: expressions as data](../features/FEA-024.md), [FEA-008: expressive program models](../features/FEA-008.md). *Relates to* [CAP-100: six languages' expression dialects](CAP-100.md), [CAP-098: dialects over one framework](CAP-098.md).

## Design

Built through stage 5 (below) and released in mbse-expressions 0.8.0 (v0.8.0, at ff7c762, PR #26), with the
namespace rename and on mbse-schemas 0.26.10; stage 6 is not, and comes in 0.10.0, after domains as data's 0.9.0
(Decided, "0.8.0 holds stages 1 to 5" and "The releases"). Domains as data, released in 0.9.0, changed the model
under every dialect: [CAP-097: value domains of published standards](CAP-097.md#value-domains),
[CAP-098: dialects over one framework](CAP-098.md#domains-as-data-in-the-framework),
[CAP-267: C and C++ terms read under the ABI their holder names](CAP-267.md) and
[CAP-268: standards as tagged data](CAP-268.md), which built this capability's "each kind recording as data the
standards that have it" (Decided, below). The framework's rules for complete dialects, which each dialect's own capability applies:
[CAP-227: the Python3 expression dialect](CAP-227.md), [CAP-228: the TypeScript5 expression dialect](CAP-228.md),
[CAP-229: the C expression dialect](CAP-229.md), [CAP-230: the C++ expression dialect](CAP-230.md) and
[CAP-231: the SystemVerilog expression dialect, complete](CAP-231.md). Today's dialects, which hold the terms of
constraints, a fraction of each language's expressions, are [CAP-100: six languages' expression dialects](CAP-100.md)'s.

Proposed and decided in [mbse-expressions' proposal, complete expression dialects](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/dialects/README.md)
(branch `complete-dialects`), which is stage 1 of [mbse-programs' port](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-programs/blob/main/docs/proposals/port/README.md#stages-proposed-once-decided)
(branch `port-schemas`); its survey of what each dialect lacks is [the gaps, dialect by dialect](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/dialects/GAPS.md).
Its stages, each passing both gates at 100% in both languages with the corpora regenerated:

1. The decisions recorded: this capability and the five above.
2. The framework and the renames, with today's kinds: `Expressions.Framework.Term` a union of each dialect's union
   (`Expressions.<Dialect>.Term`), the invalid-syntax kinds,
   `Python3`, `C` and `Cpp` from `Ccpp`'s kinds, `SystemVerilog`; the translators retargeted; corpus cases per dialect.
3. `Python3` complete ([CAP-227: the Python3 expression dialect](CAP-227.md)). Built: its
   [As built](CAP-227.md#as-built-mbse-expressions).
4. `TypeScript5`, new ([CAP-228: the TypeScript5 expression dialect](CAP-228.md)). Built: its
   [As built](CAP-228.md#as-built-mbse-expressions).
5. `C` and `Cpp` ([CAP-229: the C expression dialect](CAP-229.md), [CAP-230: the C++ expression dialect](CAP-230.md)).
   Built: their [As built](CAP-229.md#as-built-mbse-expressions) and [As built](CAP-230.md#as-built-mbse-expressions).
   Then the namespace rename (Decided, below), a step of its own before stage 6's build.
6. `SystemVerilog` complete ([CAP-231: the SystemVerilog expression dialect, complete](CAP-231.md)), and the retrofit
   of the other dialects; released as 0.10.0 (Decided, "The releases").
7. Tutorials, skill, README and EQUIVALENCE, which followed each stage as it landed; the releases: 0.8.0 after stage
   5, a breaking change; 0.9.0, domains as data, between them; and 0.10.0 after stage 6 and the retrofit (Decided,
   "0.8.0 holds stages 1 to 5" and "The releases"). Then mbse-programs' stage 2, mbse-patterns and
   mbse-codegen-python.

## Open questions

- **A literal's spelling against its value** (question 2): settled, by the user's decision that a literal is a domain
  representation, carrying its spelling beside its value ([CAP-233: literals of any value](CAP-233.md#decided), Decided).

## Decided

As the user decided, adopted, each the rule as stated, quoted as written. On mbse-programs' port (2026-11-02,
[its Decided](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-programs/blob/main/docs/proposals/port/README.md#decided)), and on mbse-expressions' proposal
(2026-11-02, [its Decided](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/dialects/README.md#decided), questions 2 to 9, and 2026-11-03, in building its stage 4):

- **One dialect per language and major version, covering its minor versions or standards.** "language dialects each
  of which supports a major version of a target language and all of the minor versions in it, such as Python3 or
  Typescript5. This means each language dialect must correctly handle the closure of syntaxes across minor versions
  such as 3.11 - 3.14." For C and C++, first "Maybe C11, C17, C23 Cpp17, cpp20, cpp23" (tentative; C17 adds no syntax
  over C11), then, on question 3: "it sounds like having 6 different dialects is overkill. what would you recommend?",
  and to the recommendation, "yes": two dialects, `C` and `Cpp`, each covering every standard of its language (C11 to
  C23, C++17 to C++23), each kind recording as data the standards that have it, as mbse-programs' standards do
  (`SINCE`) and as `SystemVerilog` records Verilog; separate, since C++ is not a superset of C; one union each, so that
  no union is completed by extension; whether a construct is in a standard a constraint, as validation is (below). And
  "yes keep verilog as a subset of systemverilog, presuming that is how the standards are partitioned" (it is: IEEE
  1800-2009 merged IEEE 1364). The dialects are `Python3`, `TypeScript5`, `C` (C11 to C23), `Cpp` (C++17 to C++23)
  and `SystemVerilog` (Verilog its subset).
- **Complete over the language's AST, gaps filled in mbse-expressions.** "`mbse-programs` should make use of
  `mbse-expressions.Dialects.<lang_ver>.Expression`. Yes, this means the expression dialect must completely cover the
  AST. Rather than using schema-extension, if there are any gaps in the dialect as modeled in
  `mbse-expressions.Dialects.<lang_ver>`, they should be added into `mbse-expressions`. There is no reason for an
  expression dialect to exist that is ia subset of the corresponding node of AST in the real langauge."
- **mbse-programs' kinds, names and shapes** (question 2, "yes to both"): its kinds, which follow each language's
  parser (`ast` for Python, typescript-estree for TypeScript), so that its stage 2 maps them one to one; ours that are
  narrower replaced by them (a comparison chain, `and` of n operands, comprehensions of several `for`s, calls with
  keywords, one `Subscript`), their evaluators and translators following; what has no counterpart in the grammar
  (Python's `import` and `importfrom` in an expression, `let` as `(lambda name: body)(value)`) kept, marked as an
  extension, as mbse-programs marks JSX; and concrete syntax (`Parenthesized`, a literal's spelling, adjacent strings)
  kept, so that source prints back as written.
- **`Expressions.Framework.Term` is a union of the dialects' unions; each dialect has its own holder.** First, asked
  whether each dialect has its own holder, the user answered "3.", naming
  `mbse-expressions.Dialects.<lang_ver>.Expression` ("Expression terms are value objects. An instance of
  OfSchemas.Expression holds the expression root."). Then, asked about the `<dialect>_` branch prefixes ("I don't
  understand the `python_` prefix"; "I don't know that we need `Expressions.Framework.Term`"): "OK if instead we keep
  `Expressions.Framework.Term`, that can be a union of `Expressions.Dialects.<lang_ver>.Expression` which is itself a
  union." and, shown the consequences, "yes". `Expressions.Framework.Term`'s branches are the dialects (`python3`,
  `typescript5`, `c`, `cpp`, `systemverilog`, `patterns`, `matlab`, `excel`, `latex`), each branch's schema the
  dialect's union of its kinds, `Expressions.<Dialect>.Term` (first recorded as `Expressions.<Dialect>.Expression`,
  revised below), whose branches are named plainly (`binop`): no prefix, each name unique where it is defined. A
  dialect's own kinds name its union in their term slots; the framework's shared kinds name `Expressions.Framework.Term`, and each dialect's union lists the shared kinds it
  reuses. Where `Framework.Term` is held, a term is written with its dialect, then its kind, as a restricted `OfAny` is
  ([CAP-069: wire format shorthand (scalars)](CAP-069.md)). Revised (2026-11-03), in
  [the proposal's Decided, 1](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/dialects/README.md#decided):
  "The root of an expression tree is always an `Expression` which holds the root `Term` as a property", and to the
  model restated, "yes it does": each dialect's holder, `Expressions.<Dialect>.Expression`, is a reference object
  holding its root `Term` as `root`, and `Expressions.Framework.Term` is the union of the dialects' `Term`s. A union
  of unions validates, builds and round-trips in mbse-schemas 0.26.5 as it stands.
- **The framework's kinds with term slots stay shared** (question 4): "validation will be by constraints not by
  schema". `let`, `call`, `list` and `mapping` stay shared, as decided on 2026-10-30
  ([CAP-100: six languages' expression dialects](CAP-100.md#decided), Decided), their slots any dialect's term; that a
  dialect's expression holds only its own terms is a constraint, not the schema's closure (option (ii)).
- **Bodies within expressions are not part of the term** (question 5, "(i)"): a function's, an arrow's or a lambda's
  body is not; statements are mbse-programs' owned reference objects ("Files, scopes, and statements should be owned
  reference objects"), and mbse-programs relates a body's statements to what holds the expression.
- **The sublanguages an expression holds are the dialect's** (question 5, as recommended, (a)): types, patterns, JSX,
  and SystemVerilog's sequences and properties, each a union of its own beside the dialect's terms.
- **A schema is a branch of every union whose positions hold it** (2026-11-03, "(b) as you recommended"). Asked how a
  property holds a kind from two unions, where mbse-programs types it as a mix of a term kind and a sublanguage's kind
  (in `TypeScript5`: an arrow's parameter `x`, a term; a type's entity name, `Identifier | ThisExpression |
  TSQualifiedName`), and shown (a) a `term` branch in each sublanguage's union, holding `Term`, a level of wrapping on
  the wire, (b) one schema listed as a branch of several unions, (c) a union of its own for each mixed position, or (d)
  the pattern kinds in `Term`, the user chose (b): a sublanguage's union lists, beside its own kinds, the term kinds its
  positions hold, each directly, with no wrapping level; which terms are allowed where beyond the schema is a
  constraint, consistent with question 4's. How `TypeScript5` applies it, and the user's "yes, stay `Identifier`
  terms" for names that only declare, are [CAP-228: the TypeScript5 expression dialect](CAP-228.md#decided)'s.
- **Every dialect has a `Pattern` union for its binding targets** (2026-11-03, "(ii)"). Asked "is this done
  consistently across typescript5 and python3? can it be?", and shown (i) each dialect following its own grammar
  (`TypeScript5` a `Pattern` union, `Python3`'s targets holding its `Term`) or (ii) a uniform rule where every dialect
  has a `Pattern` union for binding targets, the user: "(ii)". Each dialect's `Expressions.<Dialect>.Pattern` lists
  the kinds a binding target may be, one schema in several unions as (b) has it, and its binding targets hold it:
  `TypeScript5`'s ([CAP-228: the TypeScript5 expression dialect](CAP-228.md#as-built-mbse-expressions)) and
  `Python3`'s ([CAP-227: the Python3 expression dialect](CAP-227.md#decided)) are built.
- **The model and printers here, the parsers mbse-programs'** (question 6, "yes fix that phrase", (b)): mbse-programs
  reads the native parser's tree into its owned statements and the dialect's terms in one walk; completeness is checked
  here against each grammar's node list, kept as data (`ast`'s, a grammar's `node-types.json`, slang's), every node a
  kind or listed as not an expression. Python's `Text.FromText` stays until mbse-programs reads `Python3`, then moves
  there. mbse-programs' stage 1 no longer says "parsing ... complete". The native parser is mbse-programs': "`mbse-programs`
  typically uses a "native parser" or "tree walker" that conforms to and tracks evolution of publish standards. To the
  degree that the chosen "native parser" supports parsing and preserving invalid syntax, `mbse-programs` should also."
- **`error` and `missing` kinds for invalid syntax** (question 7, "agreed"): two framework kinds, every dialect's
  union holding them, modeled now and written later by parsers that keep them: `error`, what the parser skipped and the
  terms it recovered within it, and `missing`, the kind the parser expected (tree-sitter's `ERROR` and `MISSING`,
  slang's skipped and missing tokens). CPython's `ast` keeps nothing, so `Python3` read by it refuses, by position.
- **A shallow evaluator** (question 8, "agreed we can keep a shallow evalluator in mbse-expressions"): a new kind is
  validated, printed, stored and translated where a rule exists, but refused by evaluation by name ("Python3 Lambda is
  not evaluated"), each evaluator's growth a follow-up capability; what is evaluated today keeps working, the narrower
  kinds replaced keeping what they evaluate.
- **Basic is renamed Patterns, and a quantifier takes a pattern** (2026-11-02): "former `Expressions.Dialects.Basic`
  should be renamed `Expressions.Dialects.Patterns`. Any quantiier should take a Schemas.OfPattern as its sole
  argument." (First written "a Schemas.OfPredicate", which mbse-schemas no longer has; corrected by the user.) How a
  quantifier reads its pattern is proposed in [the proposal](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/dialects/README.md#decided).
- **Patterns (formerly Basic), Matlab, Excel and Latex otherwise unchanged; the translators retargeted** (question 9, "yes"): those four keep their
  kinds, changed only as the names and the holders change every dialect's install. The translators keep what they
  translate today, retargeted to the renamed dialects; a new kind has no counterpart and is refused by name. Basic and
  C, and Basic and C++, are "one rule set each over the kinds a family shares, applied to each standard" (written
  before question 3 made `C` and `Cpp` one dialect each). `TypeScript5` has no translator yet.
- **The namespace: `Expressions.<family>.<dialect>`** (decided in stage 6's decisions; the questions it left open,
  2026-11-05, [the proposal's Decided](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/dialects/README.md#decided)).
  The user chose "the middle path: `Expressions.<family>.<dialect>`, a family named as its standard names the
  language, each dialect recording the standard that defines it", and, to the names `Expressions.Python.Python3`,
  `Expressions.TypeScript.TypeScript5`, `Expressions.C.Expressions`, `Expressions.SystemVerilog.Expressions` and
  sublanguages by what they are: "agreed"; "The rename is a step of its own, before stage 6's build." To eight
  questions it left open, each with a recommendation, the user: "yes, go": Patterns is
  `Expressions.Framework.Patterns` (*superseded* 2026-11-09 by the user's "(b)": Patterns is
  `Expressions.Patterns.Expressions`, [CAP-098: dialects over one framework](CAP-098.md#decided), "Patterns' place");
  C++'s family is `Cpp` (`Expressions.Cpp.Expressions`); a family with one
  unversioned dialect names it `Expressions` (`Expressions.MATLAB.Expressions`, `Expressions.Excel.Expressions`,
  `Expressions.LaTeX.Expressions`); `Expressions.Framework.Term`'s branch tags stay (`patterns`, `python3`, `c`, ...),
  for now; a singleton is named by its full path; the modules are at `mbse/Expressions/<Family>/<Dialect>/` and
  `src/<Family>/<Dialect>/`; and there is no alias for the old names. So `Expressions.<Dialect>.Term` and
  `Expressions.<Dialect>.Expression`, above, are each dialect's `Term` and `Expression` under its path
  (`Expressions.Python.Python3.Term`).
- **0.8.0 holds stages 1 to 5; stage 6 and the retrofit move to 0.9.0** (2026-11-05,
  [the proposal's Decided](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/dialects/README.md#decided)).
  Offered two options, "(a): Release 0.8.0 now. It contains stages 1 to 5, the rename and a pin to 0.26.10. Stage 6
  and the retrofit of the other dialects come in 0.9.0, and INI-040 can start now." and "(b): build stage 6 first",
  the user answered "(a)". So 0.8.0 is released after stage 5: stages 1 to 5, the namespace rename (the old `Dialects`
  names gone, with no alias) and mbse-schemas 0.26.10 pinned. `SystemVerilog` is released in 0.8.0 as it stands, not
  yet complete to IEEE 1800-2023. Stage 6 (SystemVerilog's expression dialect complete, Verilog its subset, its types,
  patterns, assertions, constraints and preprocessor dialects, and the closing work) and the retrofit of the other
  dialects (type, pattern, regex and preprocessor dialects, `let` variations) are released as 0.9.0, *revised*: as
  0.10.0 (The releases, below). It supersedes stage 7's release of 0.8.0 after stage 6.
- **When domains as data is taken up** (2026-11-05): asked "What should I start next?", the user chose "Domains-as-data
  (Recommended)": after 0.8.0, before stage 6 and mbse-programs' port, as recommended, since domains change the model
  under every dialect, so that mbse-programs ports once.
- **The releases** (2026-11-06, on mbse-expressions' domains as data, stage 1's design, D): "agreed": domains as data is
  released as 0.9.0; stage 6 of the complete dialects and the retrofit of the other dialects as 0.10.0.
- **On process** (2026-11-10, the user, during domains as data): "I am less concerned about the number of lines of code
  and more concerned about how slugging our progress has become. It seems like we are stopping more often, halting on
  minutiae that are already answered in docs that mbse-basis/docs/README.md marks as mandatory, and losing sight of what
  is important for increased effort." So agents decide minutiae themselves (names, placements, message wording, which
  list a type goes in, test layout) from the mandatory documents and the Decided sections, mark each *(agent)* and list
  them in the pull request; they pause only on a design fork the record does not settle, where a wrong guess costs real
  rework, or on a sibling's defect, committing and pushing first. And: "make sure all agents keep the full mandtory
  reading list refreshed after every compaction": after any compaction an agent re-reads mbse-basis' `AGENTS.md`, the
  four documents it marks mandatory, the repository's `AGENTS.md` and the Decided sections of the items it works on
  before more work.
