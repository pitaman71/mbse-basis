# CAP-242: incremental physical placement

*Implements* [FEA-056: fungible diagramming keeps agents and humans in the loop](../features/FEA-056.md).

Physical layout, in TypeScript only, resolves orientations to absolute directions, places points so that every
vector's rotation and length hold, sizes each cell, container and the diagram just enough to hold what it contains,
with a collapsing margin, and enforces the minimum shape size, one mutation at a time.

## As built

mbse-diagrams' `Framework.Placement`, in TypeScript only, built by
[pull request 7](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-diagrams/pull/7) as [the diagrams proposal](../../proposals/DIAGRAMS.md) states it, with what
Decided by an agent, below, fixes where it is silent. It writes [CAP-241: physical layout schemas](CAP-241.md)'s annotations, which both
languages read, and adds `margin` and `minimum_size` to the diagram's properties, in both. Suite PLC tests it, in
TypeScript.

## Decided

The user's decisions (2026-11-06), on physical placement:

- **TypeScript only**: "`mbse-diagrams` will provide physical layout components in typescript5 only", a deliberate
  difference per [PRI-040: deliberate differences listed](../PRINCIPLES.md#PRI-040).
- **Margin, in this capability**: "`margin : float` number of units of collapsing margin between cells and between a
  container and its parent. Margin is passed from parent to child container without scaling."
- **Incremental**: the algorithm "processes mutations rather than the model itself"; at each one it makes "incremental
  adjustments to point placements that will restore consistency of the point positions with the layout rules".
- **Its rules**, from the proposal: cells added or removed expand or shrink their container's physical size; a
  container that expands or shrinks makes its parent, which may be a cell, expand or shrink "just enough to contain
  it"; shape context (orientation, `unit_length`) is computed recursively from the diagram; border points are spaced
  from their shape's origin correctly; and the minimum shape size is configurable and enforced.

## Decided by an agent, for review

Where this capability and the proposal were silent, the simplest default consistent with them, with
[CAP-237: logical layout schemas](CAP-237.md) to [CAP-241: physical layout schemas](CAP-241.md)'s decisions, each *proposed* until reviewed, labeled with the
repository and number it was decided under.

- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Names.** `Framework.Placement`: `place(container)`, after a change of a container's grid;
  `reshape(shape)`, after a change of a shape's points or vectors; `unit_length(x)`. Each returns its adjustments, the
  frames and placements that moved or resized, as data, in order, and runs within the mutation's transaction.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **A point** lies the length of the vector from its shape's origin to it, times the shape's unit
  length, along its compass, north toward −y. A point no vector from the origin reaches lies at the origin; a vector
  with no length reaches no farther.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **A shape's box** is the bounding box of its origin and its extent points, or of its border
  points where it has none, grown to hold its own grid, centred on its origin, and to `minimum_size`, 0 unless set, in
  units of its level, in width and height.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **A grid** makes each column as wide as its widest cell and each row as tall as its tallest; a
  margin separates cells, and cells from the grid's edge, never doubled; an empty grid has no size. A shared cell
  stacks its shapes along its grid's axis, a margin between each; each shape, or the stack, is centred in its cell. A
  container's grid and cells share its shape's unit length. The diagram is its grid's size, its top left (0, 0).
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Margin** is the same number of diagram units at every level, 0 unless set: unscaled, as
  decided, it is not multiplied by a level's unit length.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Moving only what must move.** A change lays out again its container's grid, then each grid
  above it only while the one below changed size, then gives absolute coordinates to everything beneath the highest
  grid laid out again; a frame or placement is written and reported only if it moved or resized. Coordinates are
  rounded to a billionth of a unit, so that trigonometry leaves no noise.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **What physical layout writes**, CAP-241's open question: both, every placement's and frame's
  absolute and relative coordinates, as data.
