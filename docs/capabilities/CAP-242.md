# CAP-242: incremental physical placement

*Implements* [FEA-056: fungible diagramming keeps agents and humans in the loop](../features/FEA-056.md).

Physical layout, in both languages, resolves orientations to absolute directions, places points so that every
vector's rotation and length hold, sizes each cell, container and the diagram just enough to hold what it contains,
with a collapsing margin, and enforces the minimum shape size, one mutation at a time.

## As built

mbse-diagrams' `Framework.Placement`, in Python and TypeScript, built by
[pull request 7](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-diagrams/pull/7) as [the diagrams proposal](../../proposals/DIAGRAMS.md) states it, with what
Decided by an agent, below, fixes where it is silent: the rules translated into linear constraints that Kiwi, an
incremental Cassowary solver, solves. It writes [CAP-241: physical layout schemas](CAP-241.md)'s annotations, which both
languages read, and adds `margin` and `minimum_size` to the diagram's properties, in both. Suite PLC tests it, in
TypeScript.

## Decided

The user's decisions (2026-11-06), on physical placement:

- **TypeScript only**, superseded on 2026-11-09, below: "`mbse-diagrams` will provide physical layout components in
  typescript5 only", a deliberate difference per [PRI-040: deliberate differences listed](../PRINCIPLES.md#PRI-040).
- **Margin, in this capability**: "`margin : float` number of units of collapsing margin between cells and between a
  container and its parent. Margin is passed from parent to child container without scaling."
- **Incremental**: the algorithm "processes mutations rather than the model itself"; at each one it makes "incremental
  adjustments to point placements that will restore consistency of the point positions with the layout rules".
- **Its rules**, from the proposal: cells added or removed expand or shrink their container's physical size; a
  container that expands or shrinks makes its parent, which may be a cell, expand or shrink "just enough to contain
  it"; shape context (orientation, `unit_length`) is computed recursively from the diagram; border points are spaced
  from their shape's origin correctly; and the minimum shape size is configurable and enforced.

The user's decisions (2026-11-09), after a first, bespoke placement algorithm was reviewed:

- **Kiwi, behind an engine-neutral layer**: "Okay let's aim for Kiwi. Assume there may be support for other layout
  engines in the future." Logical and container layout stay separate and feed it: physical layout translates them, and
  the rules, into the clauses the engine solves.
- **In Python too**: "Porting kiwi physical layout to python seems like a worthwhile expenditure." Python's engine is
  Kiwi itself, kiwisolver 1.5.1; TypeScript's, @lume/kiwi 0.4.4, its port. Moving the trace viewer, or other tools,
  from Python to TypeScript is deferred.

## Decided by an agent, for review

Where this capability and the proposal were silent, the simplest default consistent with them, with
[CAP-237: logical layout schemas](CAP-237.md) to [CAP-241: physical layout schemas](CAP-241.md)'s decisions, each *proposed* until reviewed, labeled with the
repository and number it was decided under.

- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Names.** `Framework.Constraints`: a `Constraint`, data, linear terms over named variables,
  a constant, an operator and a strength (`required`, `strong`, `medium`, `weak`); an `Engine`, which adds and removes
  constraints by id, keeping its solution; and a `ConstraintSet`, which keeps an engine in step with what the rules
  give. `Framework.Kiwi`: `KiwiEngine`, over [kiwisolver](https://github.com/nucleic/kiwi) 1.5.1 in Python and
  [@lume/kiwi](https://github.com/lume/kiwi) 0.4.4 in TypeScript, pinned, mbse-diagrams' one dependency beyond
  mbse-schemas in each. `Framework.Placement`: `Layout`, one per diagram, and `unit_length(x)`.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Driven by mutations.** After each mutation, `Layout.follow` is given the objects it touched;
  before one that removes a shape, `Layout.removing` is given the shape. It translates again only the shapes, or the
  diagram, those objects touch (the shape a point, vector, segment or spec belongs to, the container a grid or cell
  belongs to, and a shape's container), and their children where their unit length or turn changed; a change of the
  diagram's tuning touches everything. Only constraints that changed are removed from the engine and added again, and
  the engine re-solves from its last solution.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **What it writes is a mutation.** `follow` returns an upsert transaction expression writing
  each frame and placement whose values changed, each found by its owner's path from the diagram directory, which the
  caller runs and may journal; or null where nothing moved. This answers the user's question why the first algorithm
  returned native data rather than mutations.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **The rules as constraints.** A point is a constant offset from its shape's origin: the length
  of the vector to it, times the shape's unit length, along its compass, north toward −y; a point no vector from the
  origin reaches lies at the origin, and a vector with no length reaches no farther. A shape's content is the least
  box (`weak`) holding its origin, its extent points, or its border points where it has none, and its grid, centred on
  its origin; its frame the least box (`weak`) holding its content and `minimum_size`, in units of its level, centred
  on its content. A grid's columns follow each other a margin apart, the first and last a margin from its edge, each
  the least width (`weak`) holding its cells, and rows likewise; a cell's shapes are stacked along its grid's axis, a
  margin between each, the stack centred along it and each shape across it; an empty grid has no size; a container's
  grid and cells share its unit length. The diagram's grid is at (0, 0), and the diagram is its size.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Every removal before any addition.** Within one mutation, every touched shape is translated
  first; then every constraint that went, or whose form changed, is removed; and only then is what is new added. Kiwi,
  the C++ original and its port alike, holding a constraint beside the one replacing it, which a third makes linearly
  dependent on it, can leave the survivor unmet once the other goes, though it reports it held: the seeded sequence
  found this, reduced to 15 additions and one removal.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **The two languages agree**, value for value, on every frame and placement each mutation
  writes, in every PLC case, the seeded sequence included: Kiwi's two implementations differ only in floating-point
  noise, which rounding to a millionth absorbs.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Unique solutions.** Every preference is `weak` and pulls a size or extent toward its least,
  which the required constraints make unique, so that what the engine finds does not depend on the order it was told
  things: a layout followed mutation by mutation agrees with one made afresh.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Segments are no constraint.** A segment places neither of its points, so it adds nothing to
  the engine; its angle and length follow from where its points are placed. Rules on segments themselves, an angle or
  a length between two free points, would not be linear.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Rounding.** Kiwi solves in doubles and rounds nothing. A value moved by less than a millionth
  of a unit has not moved, and values are written rounded to a millionth; a layout followed mutation by mutation and
  one made afresh agree to within two millionths, since a value half way between two may round either way. Replaying a
  journal reproduces it exactly.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Conflicts.** A required constraint the engine refuses is ignored and queued, the later of
  two that conflict, and the queue is tried again, in order, whenever a constraint is removed or changed: the physical
  half of [CAP-240: conflicting layout rules ignored and requeued](CAP-240.md)'s rule, which none of the rules built so far can trigger.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **Margin** is the same number of diagram units at every level, 0 unless set: unscaled, as
  decided. Between a shape's frame and its own cells there are two margins, its parent cell's and its own grid's; they
  do not collapse into one.
- **(mbse-diagrams 7)** **What physical layout writes**, CAP-241's open question: both, every placement's and frame's
  absolute and relative coordinates, as data.

## Open questions

- **Cost.** At about 220 shapes, a mutation costs about 24 ms to follow and 15 ms to run what it writes, most of it
  constraints removed and added again, and recording and running the upsert; the solve itself is under 1%. Absolute
  coordinates make a growing shape move everything after it, about 15 frames and placements a mutation.
- **Whether the queue of ignored physical constraints belongs in the store**, as `Container`'s rules do, rather than
  in memory.
