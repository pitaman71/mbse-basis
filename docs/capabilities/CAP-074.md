# CAP-074: transforms applied step by step

*Implements* [FEA-059: transforms of models, applied step by step and certified](../features/README.md#FEA-059), [FEA-021: every generation ambiguity a parameter](../features/FEA-021.md), [FEA-017: AI in the loop via the model trace](../features/README.md#FEA-017), [FEA-018: humans in the loop via the model trace](../features/README.md#FEA-018).

A transform has a prototype, which says what it applies to, a procedure, which applies it, and a certificate, which
records each application. A session applies transforms one per step, each to up to three frames (a source, a sync and a
target), a policy ranking the candidates and answering parameters, and every application certified
([CAP-075: steps linked to what they matched and wrote](CAP-075.md)). This is what mbse-transforms' first release builds
([its first-release proposal](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-transforms/blob/main/docs/proposals/first-release/README.md)),
for mbse-codegen-python.

## Design

What the user decided is stated under Decided, below; the rest is mbse-transforms' first-release proposal, each part
*proposed* until reviewed (Decided by an agent, for review).

### The model

- **A transform** (`Transforms.Transform`) is schema data, installed by `install(txn)`: its name; its **prototype**, an
  `OfPattern` of mbse-schemas; its **parameters**, mbse-schemas' Parametrics ([CAP-112: parameters modeled as schema data](CAP-112.md)),
  each typed by a schema, its value domain; and the name under which its procedure is registered.
- **A procedure** is a native function, registered by name, `procedure(source, sync, target, arguments)`. Each frame is
  a `Visitors.OfPattern` of mbse-schemas, or absent where the transform has none. The procedure reads and writes its
  frames through mbse-schemas' visitors, and it does not know how the copies behind them are made.
- **An application** is given frames that are already bound. It runs the procedure, verifies the result where the
  transform asks it to (the prototype falsified for the frames, or a progress metric decreased), and writes the
  application's certificates ([CAP-075: steps linked to what they matched and wrote](CAP-075.md)).
- **A session** is the caller of applications, which the procedure does not see. It finds the prototype's matches in
  the source, each a candidate; it binds the frames, makes or reuses the copies, applies one transform per step, and
  keeps the history certificate. A session makes its copies in its own way: a stacked store over the source
  ([CAP-308: stacking by mutation transactions, in one round trip](README.md#CAP-308)) keeps the source undisturbed with
  the edits above it, and a snapshot does the same.
- **A policy** ranks candidates and answers open parameters, as ordered clauses (the precursor's `Policy` and
  `Clause`); it never makes a candidate. Who decided each step's parameters (the caller, a policy, or an earlier history
  reused) is recorded by the history certificate ([CAP-076: reruns reuse decisions](CAP-076.md)).
- **A candidate** is a match of the prototype with arguments: a value for each parameter from its domain. Where a domain
  is finite (a union of options, a `bool`, a bounded int), each value is a candidate of its own. Where a domain is not
  finite (a string, an unbounded int), the candidate has an open parameter, which a person, an agent or a policy
  answers.

Variables of a specification and parameters of a step are distinct (mbse-schemas' Parametrics: a parameter is a
variable whose binder is a schema). A step's parameters are the *transform's* variables; a parametric schema's
parameters are the *model's*, which a transform carries into the target as the target language's own construct (a C++
template parameter). Documents and messages say "step parameter" or "model parameter" wherever both are near.

### Data

Transforms, their prototypes and parameters, and the certificates are mbse-schemas data, installed, so that they are
written, read, validated and compared as any other data, and both implementations write them byte-identically. The
history certificate is a singleton's or an owner's, and every certificate is owned under it, so that each certificate
has an object reference ([CAP-315: every read within a transaction or through a follow](CAP-315.md)). The certificates
are [CAP-075: steps linked to what they matched and wrote](CAP-075.md)'s.

## As built: mbse-patterns

mbse-patterns 0.7 to 0.9.1 built transforms as the module `Transforms`, with the TRF suites and tutorial 8: a transform
was a `before` and an `after` over mbse-patterns' predicates, with a rewrite function; a `Session` took one decision per
step, by the caller or by a `Policy` of `Clause`s, and kept the steps as a `Transforms.Trace`. That precursor is the
design input of mbse-transforms, not code to port. It is retired: mbse-patterns 0.10.0, its fresh start, removes its
code, and mbse-codegen-python stays pinned to mbse-patterns 0.9.1 until it moves to mbse-transforms.

## Open questions

- **Undecided matches.** A match whose prototype's check is unknown (three-valued) is reported, not a candidate. Should
  a caller be able to apply a transform there anyway, as a decision recorded like any other?

- **Speculation.** Bounded speculation (taking several alternatives in scoped sub-histories and keeping the best by a
  costing function scoped to them) needs sub-histories and undo. How are scopes declared, and how does a costing
  function relate to a policy's ranking?

- **Enumerated domains.** A domain of options is a union of described branches, which needs no new machinery but is
  verbose on the wire (a union value of an empty value object). An enumeration of literals needs constraints on a
  native, which are planned here. Which comes first?

## Decided

As the user decided: adopted, each the rule as stated, quoted where the wording matters.

- **Transforms in a repository of their own**, mbse-transforms: "yes transforms goes in its own repo"
  ([mbse-patterns' charter](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-patterns/blob/main/docs/proposals/rescope/README.md);
  [mbse-transforms' charter](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-transforms/blob/main/docs/proposals/charter/README.md)).
  It is a new package, at 0.1.0 (its charter, Decided, 1), depending on mbse-schemas for patterns, parameters, stores
  and transactions, and on mbse-programs for program trees, and it is built after mbse-programs' port. Its precursor,
  mbse-patterns 0.9.1's `Transforms` (As built, above), is the design input, not code to port.
- **A transform has a prototype, a procedure, and a certificate**
  ([the first-release proposal](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-transforms/blob/main/docs/proposals/first-release/README.md#the-transform-model-as-the-user-decided)):
  "The prototype comprises what you called `before`. The procedure is native code for the time being." A transform may
  be parametric.
- **The procedure is native code for the time being**: "The procedure is native code for the time being." It supersedes
  the decision of mbse-transforms' charter (Decided, 2) that a rewrite is a mutation expression of mbse-schemas
  ([FEA-027: mutations as queries](../features/FEA-027.md)), as the user decided ("yes", to that decision being
  superseded by the procedure). A procedure reads and writes its frames through mbse-schemas: "No model should need
  bespoke schema-aware code to read or write. Use mbse-schemas built-in serialization support."
  ([CAP-282: no model needs bespoke schema-aware code to read or write](README.md#CAP-282)). Procedures as data wait on mbse-schemas
  evaluating expressions ([CAP-142: expressions evaluated in queries](CAP-142.md)).
- **Application takes up to three frames**, each a `Visitors.OfPattern` of mbse-schemas:
  - "`source` - a read-only `Visitors.OfPattern` bearing a pattern frame bound to a source copy that is not to be
    disturbed (nondestructive)";
  - "`sync` - a writeable `Visitors.OfPattern` bearing a pattern frame bound to a copy that is to be edited in place
    (destructive)";
  - "`target` - a writeable `Visitors.OfPattern` bearing a pattern frame bound to a (usually empty) copy that is to be
    replaced (constructive)".

  "The transforms application procedure does not need to know how copies are made since it takes Visitors.OfPattern as
  an argument."
- **A pattern instance is a frame**: "a pattern instance is a frame".
- **Every application is certified**, at three levels, which "form a complete audit history for transforms"
  ([CAP-075: steps linked to what they matched and wrote](CAP-075.md#decided), Decided).
- **Termination is the prototype's.** A match of the prototype is a candidate, and "application may optionally verify
  that the pattern is falsified by the application or more generally an additional metric that computes recursion
  progress toward termination".
- **No decisions below the parameter level**: "Discrete decisions in a transformation procedure must themselves be
  modeled as discrete transforms with non-overlapping patterns."
- **Python first** ("yes"): each stage of mbse-transforms' first release passes in Python, and TypeScript follows as a
  stage of its own, as in mbse-programs' port.

## Decided by an agent, for review

Each is *proposed* until reviewed.

- **(mbse-transforms 3)** **The design above** where the user's decisions leave it open: a transform's model, a
  procedure's signature and its registration by name, an application's verification, the session as the caller of
  applications that binds frames and makes copies, and policies of clauses.
- **(mbse-transforms 3)** **A procedure's deterministic branches are not decisions.** A procedure's deterministic
  branches on data, which choose nothing, are computation, not decisions
  ([PRI-014: every ambiguity a decision](../PRINCIPLES.md#PRI-014)).
- **(mbse-patterns)** **No defaults** (mbse-schemas' Parametrics). A question no one answered stays open. A policy is
  not a default: the caller chooses it, and it decides only where the caller lets it; every decision that it makes is
  recorded as its own.
- **(mbse-patterns)** **One transform applied per step**, each step one decision.
- **(mbse-patterns)** **Linear resolution**: one step at a time, the candidates found again after every step, with no
  sub-histories or undo in the core; speculation comes later.
- **(mbse-patterns)** **Parameters are typed by a value domain**, a schema (`OfParameter`'s type), usually but not only
  a native, so that a policy can weigh and propose values.
- **(mbse-patterns)** **Determinism** (the opening of [mbse-schemas'
  design](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-schemas/blob/main/README.md): bindings "can be automatically and
  deterministically generated"). The same models, policies and parameters give the same applications, the same
  certificates and the same result, in every implementation. Candidates are ranked in a canonical order (by transform,
  then by match, then by arguments in each domain's order), and nothing depends on hash order, a clock or memory
  addresses.
- **(mbse-patterns)** **Programs are trees, never text templates** (mbse-programs). A codegen procedure writes syntax
  nodes; printing is the target language's own, last.
