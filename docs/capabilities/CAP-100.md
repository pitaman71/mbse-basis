# CAP-100: six languages' expression dialects

*Implements* [FEA-024: expressions as data](../features/FEA-024.md). Index entry: [capabilities/README.md](README.md#CAP-100).

## As built: mbse-expressions

As built in 0.7.0. Decided since, and built from mbse-expressions' stage 2 on ([CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md#decided), Decided): a
union of the dialects' unions in place of one union of prefixed kinds, `C` and `Cpp` in place of `Ccpp`, `Patterns` in
place of `Basic`, and `Python3`. Where this section names the 0.7.0 forms, they are superseded.

### Dialects

Each dialect's `install(txn)` adds its kinds to `Expressions.Framework.Term` by schema extension, run after the
framework's, in a transaction of its own: a value object schema per kind, `Expressions.<Dialect>.<Kind>`, added as the
branch `<dialect>_<kind>` (`python_binop`, `latex_frac`), since every dialect adds its kinds to the one union and
several have a `binary` or a `member` (*superseded*: `Expressions.Framework.Term` is a union of the dialects' unions,
each naming its kinds plainly, so the prefix goes, [CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md#decided)). A dialect reuses the framework's kinds wherever its kind has their shape exactly:
`literal` (a `value` of the five basic scalars), `variable` (`name`), `let` (`name`, `value`, `body`) and `call`
(`callable` by name, positional `arguments`); a term carries no domain
([CAP-097: value domains of published standards](CAP-097.md#decided), Decided, stage 3). It keeps a kind of its own, `<dialect>_<kind>`, only where the shape
differs. A restriction of its own on a reused kind, such as "no bytes", is a check of its `Kind`, which validation
reports. Each dialect, kind by kind:

- **Python** reuses `literal` (its constants hold the five basic scalars), `variable` (a name; its builtins are its
  `Kind`'s ambient names, and that it is an identifier its check) and `let` (`(lambda a: body)(value)`). Its
  `python_call` stays: its callee is a term (`np.add`, an attribute of a name), where the framework's `callable` is a
  name.
- **Matlab** reuses `literal` (no bytes, its check), `variable` and `call`.
- **Excel** reuses `literal` (no bytes, its check), `variable`, `let` (`LET`) and `call` (its functions).
- **Latex** reuses `literal` (its constant: no bytes, its check), `variable` (its symbol), `call` (its function,
  whose `name` is now `callable`) and `let` (its `where`, which has a let's members).
- **Ccpp** reuses `variable` (its identifier) and `call`. Its `ccpp_constant` stays: it has C's `type`. (*Superseded*:
  `Ccpp` is refactored into `C` and `Cpp`, each covering its standards, [CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md#decided).)
- **SystemVerilog** reuses `literal` (its unsized constant: no bool, no bytes, its check), `variable` (its identifier)
  and `call`. Its `systemverilog_vector` stays: its `value` is its bits, with `signed` and `base`.

Its `DIALECT`, a `Terms.Dialect`, declares how the framework reads each kind it reads, its own and the framework's it
reuses; its writers (`Excel.Expressions.function('AND', a, b)`, ...) write them wherever a term is written. In the
table, a kind is named without its dialect's prefix.

| Dialect | Kinds | Values | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | `literal`, `call`, `variable`, `let` (the framework's), and its own `quantifier` | natives, objects, collections | three-valued (Kleene), no coercion; absent is unknown |
| Python | `constant` (the framework's `literal`), `name` (`variable`), `attribute`, `subscript` (`x['k']`), `index` (`xs[i]`), `call`, `compare` (with `in`, `not in`), `boolop`, `binop` (arithmetic and bitwise), `unaryop` (`not`, `-`, `+`, `~`), `ifexp`, `generator` (`(e for p in xs if c)`), `let` (`(lambda a: body)(value)`, the framework's), `import`, `importfrom`: Python's `ast` | Python's; a list property is a list of its values | Python's: `and`/`or` give an operand, `1 == 1.0`, `True + 1` is 2; absent attributes raise |
| Matlab | `constant` (the framework's `literal`), `identifier` (`variable`), `binary` (`==` ... `&&`, `\|\|`, `+`, `-`, `.*`), `unary` (`~`, `-`), `call` (the framework's: `isfield`, `bitand`, `bitor`, `bitxor`, `bitshift`, `all`, `any`, `nnz`, `numel`, `sum`, `min`, `max`, `unique`, `ismember`, and functions of the scope), `field` (`s.age`), `index` (`xs(i)`, from 1), `arrayfun` (`arrayfun(@(p) body, xs)`), `import` (`import pkg.fn`, `import pkg.*`) | double, logical, string, struct, arrays of them | MATLAB's: two-valued with short-circuit, logicals and doubles convert, `+` concatenates strings; the bit functions take integers from 0 to 2^53; absent fields raise |
| Excel | `constant` (the framework's `literal`), `name` (`variable`), `cell` (`A1`, `Sheet1!B2`, `[Book.xlsx]Sheet1!A1`), `let` (`LET`, the framework's), `function` (the framework's `call`: `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`, `IF` (2 or 3 arguments; `AND`, `OR`, `SUM`, `MIN` and `MAX` 1 to 255), `ISERROR`, `BITAND`, `BITOR`, `BITXOR`, `BITLSHIFT`, `BITRSHIFT`, `ROWS`, `INDEX`, `MATCH`, `ISNUMBER`, `SUM`, `MIN`, `MAX`, `UNIQUE`, and add-ins), `infix` (`=`, `<>`, `<` ... `+`, `-`, `*`), `prefix` (`-`), `field` (`r.age`), `map` (`MAP(xs, LAMBDA(p, body))`) | number, text, logical, error, record, arrays of them | Excel's: errors are values (`#FIELD!`, `#NAME?`, `#VALUE!`, `#REF!`) that propagate; `AND`/`OR` evaluate every argument, `IF` one branch; arithmetic coerces; comparisons order numbers < text < logicals and ignore case; the bit functions take integers from 0 to 2^48 - 1, else `#NUM!` |
| Ccpp | `constant` (with an optional type: `5u`, `1.5f`, `(uint8_t)5`), `identifier` (the framework's `variable`), `unary` (`+`, `-`, `!`, `~`), `binary` (`*` ... `\|\|`, with C's precedence), `conditional` (`?:`), `cast` (`(type)x`), `member` (`x.a`, `x->a`), `subscript` (`a[i]`), `call` (the framework's: functions of the scope) | C's arithmetic types under LP64 (`bool`, `char` ... `unsigned long long`, `int8_t` ... `uint64_t`, `size_t`, `float`, `double`, `long double` as IEEE 754 `binary128`), strings, structs, arrays | C's: the integer promotions and the usual arithmetic conversions; unsigned arithmetic wraps; what C leaves undefined (signed overflow, division by zero, shifts out of range, casts of floats out of range) raises; casts to integers wrap; comparisons give `bool`, as in C++; `?:` keeps its chosen operand's type |
| SystemVerilog | `constant` (the framework's `literal`: unsized integers, reals, strings), `vector` (sized literals: `8'hff`, `4'b10x1`, `8'sd5`), `identifier` (`variable`), `unary` (`+`, `-`, `!`, `~` and the reductions `&`, `~&`, `\|`, `~\|`, `^`, `~^`; its operand a primary, so `-(-x)`), `binary` (`**` ... `\|\|`, `===`, `==?`, `->`, `<->`, with IEEE 1800's precedence), `conditional`, `concatenation` (`{a, b}`), `replication` (`{n{a}}`), `select` (`a[i]`), `range` (`a[7:4]`), `inside` (with `span`s, `[lo:hi]`), `cast` (`int'(x)`, `signed'(x)`, `8'(x)`), `member`, `call` (the framework's: system functions and functions of the scope), `method` (`xs.size()`, `xs.sum()`, `xs.min()`), `iterate` (`xs.and(p) with (p > 0)`) | 4-state and 2-state vectors of any width and signedness (`logic`, `bit`, `byte` ... `longint`, `integer`, `time`), `real` and `shortreal`, structs, arrays | IEEE 1800's: operands sized by context or by themselves, extended by signedness; x and z propagate through arithmetic and comparisons, and follow the truth tables of the bitwise and logical operators; `===` and `==?` compare exactly and with wildcards; division by zero gives x; casts convert as assignments do |
| Latex | `constant` (the framework's `literal`: numbers, `\text{...}`, `\mathrm{true}`), `symbol` (`variable`: `a`, `\mathit{age}`), `binary` (`=`, `\neq`, `<` ... `\land`, `\lor`, `\implies`, `+`, `-`, `\cdot`), `unary` (`\lnot`, `-`), `frac`, `member` (`x.\mathit{age}`), `function` (the framework's `call`: `\operatorname{has}`), `where` (`let`) | number, text, truth | none: notation is written, rendered, checked and translated, and evaluated in the dialects it is translated to |

Python, Matlab, Excel, Latex, Ccpp and SystemVerilog each have a `Text` module, as every mbse framework names a
dialect's source text: `Text.ToText(term)` is their source text (`hasattr(this, 'email') if this.age >= 18 else
True`, `this.age >= 18 && isfield(this, "email")`, `=AND(this.age >= 18, NOT(ISERROR(this.email)))`,
`\mathit{this}.\mathit{age} \geq 18 \land \operatorname{has}(\mathit{this}, \text{email})`), with imports as the lines
before the expression, and constructors for their kinds (`Excel.Expressions.function('AND', a, b)`, ...). In Python, the
Python dialect's `Text.FromText(source)` reads source text back, as a writer: imports, then one expression, a chained
comparison `a < b < c` as `a < b and b < c`, `b` written in both (which writes back unchained). The Matlab and Excel
evaluators are models of those languages' rules for scalars, not calls into MATLAB or Excel, and so is the TypeScript
implementation's evaluator of Python expressions.

NumPy is not a dialect but a vocabulary of the Python dialect: `import numpy as np\nnp.greater_equal(t['age'], 18)`
is a Python expression, which evaluates vectorized, with missing values masked, when its scope allows numpy.

Each dialect resolves names, imports and references its own way, through its scope:

| Dialect | Scope | Names resolve to | Imports and functions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | `Variables(variables)` | the innermost let, then the variable | none |
| Python | `Scope(variables, modules, builtins)` | the innermost let or import, then the variable, then the builtin (`abs`, `hasattr`, `len`, ...) | `import m`, `import m as a`, `from m import n`: only a module in `modules` or a submodule of one. Private attributes (`_x`) are refused, and a call may call only a builtin, a variable's value or a function of an allowed module |
| Matlab | `Scope(variables, functions, packages)` | the variable | a function is a built-in, then imported (`import pkg.fn`, `import pkg.*` from `packages`), then on the path (`functions`), then qualified (`pkg.fn`) |
| Excel | `Workbook(names, sheets, sheet=, name=, books=, add_ins=)` | the innermost `LET`, then the defined name, else `#NAME?`; a cell is its value on its sheet and book, 0 if empty, `#REF!` if the sheet or book does not exist | a function is a built-in, then an add-in, else `#NAME?` |
| Ccpp | `Scope(variables, functions=)` | the variable, typed by its value | a function is one the scope provides |
| SystemVerilog | `Scope(variables, functions=)` | the variable, typed by its value (a `Logic` or a real) | a function is a system function (`$clog2`, `$bits`, ...), then one the scope provides |

Expressions may come from data, so nothing is imported or called unless the caller's scope provides it: an import
resolves only what the scope allowlists, never by loading code.

## Open questions

- Reading constraints from source text is mbse-programs' (Decided, below); how today's dialects' printers move to
  complete dialects is [CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md#decided)'s.

## Decided

As the user decided: adopted, each the rule as stated, quoted where the wording matters.

- **Parsing belongs in mbse-programs; no dialect gains a parser here** (2026-11-02, "yes fix that phrase", on
  mbse-expressions' [complete dialects](../../../mbse-expressions/docs/proposals/dialects/README.md#decided), question
  6; decided by an agent first, and so adopted). Parsing and printing source code belong in mbse-programs, which holds
  each language's complete syntax tree with an established parser and printer; no dialect gains a parser here, and a
  constraint in source text is read by mbse-programs and translated to a dialect. As the complete dialects refine it,
  the model and printers are mbse-expressions', the parsers mbse-programs', and Python's `Text.FromText` stays until
  mbse-programs reads `Python3`, then moves there ([CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md#decided)).

- **A dialect reuses the framework's kinds wherever the shape matches** (2026-10-30, "yes", on what the port's stage
  2b raised; [the port](../../../mbse-expressions/docs/proposals/port/README.md#decided), Decided): `literal`,
  `variable`, `let` and `call` (`callable` by name, positional `arguments`), defining a kind of its own,
  `<dialect>_<kind>`, only where its shape differs (a C constant typed by C's type; Python's call, whose callee is a
  term). (The `<dialect>_` naming is *superseded* by a union of the dialects' unions, [CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md#decided); the reuse stands, the shared
  kinds holding any dialect's term.)

## Decided by an agent, for review

Recorded in mbse-expressions' design, not marked as the user's: each *proposed* until reviewed.

- A dialect's source text is its `Text` module, as in every mbse framework, where text is one more dialect or variant:
  `Text.ToText(term)` writes it, and `Text.FromText(source)` reads it back (Python's, in Python only), replacing
  `render` and `parse`.
- SystemVerilog's integral values are 4-state (`Domains.Logic`, its bits encoded as VPI's `aval` and `bval`) whatever
  their type, a 2-state type keeping x and z at 0; evaluation sizes operands as IEEE 1800 does, rather than in Basic's
  unbounded integers. A shift by the width or more shifts every bit out, and `$bits` gives its argument's type's width
  without evaluating it. A string is a vector of its UTF-8 bytes.
- **(mbse-expressions port, API 15)** **How each dialect reuses the framework's kinds** (Decided, above): a kind is
  reused only where it has the framework kind's shape exactly, `literal` (`value`, one of the five basic scalars),
  `variable` (`name`), `let` (`name`, `value`, `body`) and `call` (`callable` by name, positional `arguments`). A restriction of the dialect's own on a reused kind is its `Kind`'s check, which
  validation reports: Matlab's and Excel's literals hold no bytes ("a literal cannot hold a bytes", "an Excel literal
  cannot hold a bytes"), nor Latex's, a new check ("a Latex literal cannot hold a bytes": its old schema took bytes
  without a word), and SystemVerilog's no bool and no bytes ("a SystemVerilog literal cannot hold a bool", was "a
  constant cannot hold a bool"); Latex's and SystemVerilog's writers write any value, as they did, and Matlab's and
  Excel's raise `TypeError`, as they did. Python's name has its builtins as its `Kind`'s ambient names, and "a name must
  be an identifier" as its check. So validation names the framework's kind where it named the dialect's ("variable 'x'
  is not bound", was "python_name 'x'", "latex_symbol 'x'", ...), and translation too ("Python literal b'\x00' has no
  Matlab counterpart", was "Python constant"). A term of a reused kind is the same value in each dialect that reuses
  it: Basic's `variable` is Python's name, so evaluating it in Python looks the name up, where it raised "Python has no
  kind 'variable'".
- **(mbse-expressions port, API 10)** (*Superseded* by a union of the dialects' unions, kinds named plainly, [CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md#decided).) A
  dialect's own kinds are branches of `Expressions.Framework.Term` named
  `<dialect>_<kind>` (the dialect lowercase: `python_binop`, `latex_frac`), their schemas `Expressions.<Dialect>.<Kind>`,
  since every dialect adds its kinds to the one union and several have a `binary`, a
  `constant` or a `member`. Each dialect's package exports its `install`, run after the framework's, in a transaction
  of its own.
