# CAP-294: POSIX's regular expressions as data

*Implements* [FEA-024: expressions as data](../features/FEA-024.md), [FEA-008: expressive program models](../features/FEA-008.md). *Relates to* [CAP-281: Python's, ECMAScript's and C++'s regular expressions as data](CAP-281.md), [CAP-267: C and C++ terms read under the ABI their holder names](CAP-267.md), [CAP-268: standards as tagged data](CAP-268.md), [CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md).

## As built: mbse-expressions

Built on mbse-expressions' branch `posix-regexp` (PR #43), in both languages, for 0.10.0. The choices made where the
decisions were silent are [the port's API, items 311 to 323](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/port/API.md),
which hold the detail this section only bridges.

- **`POSIX BRE` and `POSIX ERE`** (`Expressions.POSIX.BRE`, `Expressions.POSIX.ERE`, tags `posix_bre`, `posix_ere`):
  XBD 9.3 and 9.4's grammars, complete to XBD 9.5's, editions IEEE Std 1003.1-2001, -2008, -2017 and -2024, the ISO
  designations C++ cites (ISO/IEC 9945:2003, ISO/IEC/IEEE 9945:2009) recorded as their common names. Each reads the
  other; `Cpp RegExp` reads both ([CAP-281: Python's, ECMAScript's and C++'s regular expressions as data](CAP-281.md)).
- **grep, egrep and awk are not dialects**: POSIX defines no grammar of their own. grep reads a `pattern_list` of BREs
  (of EREs with `-E`), a kind of each dialect; awk reads EREs with C's escapes, an ERE printed for awk.
- **Tests**: the suites BRE (ten cases) and ERE (ten); FRM-01, FRM-02 and FRM-13; the corpus cases `posix_bre` and
  `posix_ere`, byte-identical in both languages.

## Decided

- **POSIX's grammars are POSIX's dialects** (2026-11-12): "yes to a new posix family", as recorded in
  [CAP-281: Python's, ECMAScript's and C++'s regular expressions as data](CAP-281.md#decided).

## Decided by an agent, for review

- **(mbse-expressions, posix-regexp, API 311 to 323)** The dialects named as POSIX abbreviates them, `BRE` and `ERE`,
  against `Basic` and `Extended`; editions that only fold in corrigenda (2004, 2013, 2016) not recorded as their own;
  what POSIX.1-2024 added inside a kind (BRE's `\?`, `\+`, `\|`; ERE's repetition modifier) not recorded per edition.
