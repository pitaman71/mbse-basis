# CAP-098: dialects over one framework

*Implements* [FEA-024: expressions as data](../features/FEA-024.md).

## As built: mbse-expressions

### The framework

`mbse.Expressions.Framework` defines what every dialect implements, and implements most of it from the dialects'
kinds, in modules: `Terms` (the framework's schemas, and the generic reads of terms), `Objects` (the data objects
expressions read), `Symbolics` (names, scopes and dependencies), `Domains`, `Evaluators`, `Partials`, `Translators` and
`Errors`. The port to mbse-schemas 0.26 settles its API in [the port's API](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/port/API.md).

- `Terms.install(txn)` defines `Expressions.Framework.Term`, a union of value object kinds (`literal`, `call`,
  `variable`, `let`, and since 0.7.0 the container constructors `list` and `mapping`), none with a domain; `Expressions.Framework.Expression`, a reference object whose `root`
  is a term; and `Expressions.Framework.HasRoot(schema, expression)`, which gives `Schemas.OfExpression` the extension
  adjacency `expression`. A dialect adds its kinds to `Term` by schema extension, in its own install, as branches
  `<dialect>_<kind>`, reusing the framework's kinds wherever its kind has their shape ([CAP-100: six languages' expression dialects](CAP-100.md#dialects)).
- Terms are read only through mbse-schemas' readers. `Terms.term(x)` gives the term value of an expression, of a term
  property or of a `Readers.OfAny` holding one; `Terms.kind_of(term)` its branch, `(tag, record)`; `Terms.form(term)`
  its `Form`: its `kind`, its `attributes` (the properties that are not terms) and its ordered `arguments` (its slots,
  properties holding a term, then the elements of a container of terms), read from the kind's schema. `walk`, `fold`
  and `same` (structural equality: natives of one type by value, NaN is NaN, -0.0 is not 0.0) work on any dialect
  through forms alone.
- `Terms.Dialect(name, kinds, domain_of=..., literal=..., extends=...)` is an expression language: the kinds it reads,
  by tag, each a `Terms.Kind`: its role, the property naming its operator, its vocabulary (operator names to
  signatures; None accepts any name, with `signature`), its required properties, and, by role, whether a reference is
  `lexical` and its `ambient` names, the names an import binds (`names(attributes)`, of its attributes), its own problems (`check(record)`)
  and its own domain (`typed(record)`). A dialect `validate`s, `infer`s and `make`s (the writer of a form) terms of its
  kinds, and `spec(x)` makes a writer of a writer, a term (copied), a native value (its literal) or a callable taking
  a term value's `Visitors.OfUnion`.
- **A dialect may extend another**: `Terms.Dialect(name, kinds, extends=base)`. Its kinds are the base's and its own,
  whose tags must differ from the base's ("Clash would declare 'literal', which Basic has"), so the extension
  validates, infers and evaluates terms that mix them; a base reports an extension's kind it does not read ("Basic has
  no kind 'twice'"). mbse-patterns 0.9.1's predicates extended Basic this way; mbse-patterns 0.10.0 holds none
  ([CAP-150: predicates as data](CAP-150.md#decided)).
- The roles are what validation, inference, evaluation and translation understand without knowing the dialect:
  `literal` (a basic scalar), `reference` (what the scope resolves: the value bound to a name, or a cell), `application`
  (an operator applied to arguments), `binding` (binds a name to its first argument within the others), `quantifier`
  (binds a name to each item of its first argument within its second, combined by its operator; its signature gives
  the items' domain, `items(domain)`) and `import` (makes what it declares available within its body). `validate`
  reports what the Basic section above lists, with the same messages in every dialect ("a literal needs a value",
  "variable 'x' is not bound", "'**' is not a core operation"), where `core` means the dialect's vocabulary.
- `Domains` defines, for inference, `Domain` (`contains(value)`, `includes(domain)`) and `Signature` (`arity()`,
  `result(domains)`, `describe()`), with generic implementations: `OfTypes`, `OfValues`, `OfUnion`, `Anything`,
  `Function`, `Same` (one of several domains, the same for every argument), `Overloaded` (the first signature whose
  parameters include the arguments', or else the union of the results of those that may apply), `Opaque` (any
  arguments, a fixed result: a call) and `Either` (gives one of its arguments, as Python's `and` does). Each dialect's
  `Domains` module declares its vocabulary's signatures over them, marked in source comments as
  [CAP-232: domains inferred for terms](CAP-232.md)'s to replace; since 0.9.0 its value domains are stored data, read
  through `Domains`' reading interface (below). `Dialect.infer(term, environment)` gives a term's domain
  from them, raising `TypeError` for an operator that cannot take its arguments' domains:
  `add cannot take (int, float); it takes (T, T) -> T for T in int | float`. Inference is permissive: `Anything`, the
  domain of a value not known statically, is accepted wherever a domain is expected, and extensions give `Anything`.
- `Symbolics` is about names. `free(term, dialect)` gives the names a term needs from its scope: its lexical
  references that no binding or import within it binds, and that are not ambient. `imports(term, dialect)` gives the
  imports it declares, which its scope must provide. `Scope` is where references are resolved at evaluation, the
  dialect's way: `lookup(reference)`, `bind(name, value)` (the scope within a binding) and `enter(declaration)` (the
  scope within an import). `Variables` is the scope of names bound to values, which dialect scopes derive from, and an
  evaluator given a mapping makes its dialect's scope from it.
- `Evaluators` defines `Evaluator` (`(term, scope) -> value`). `Interpreter` evaluates a term by its kind's role and
  calls an implementation per operator with one thunk per argument, the term and the scope, so each dialect decides
  what to evaluate and when; operators outside a closed vocabulary go to an `extension`, which is how Matlab and Excel
  call the functions their scopes provide. It raises what `validate` reports.

- `Objects` reads the data objects expressions are evaluated over, through mbse-schemas' readers: their properties
  (a container as listed values), their entries, and whether one is a reference object.
- `Errors` names the exceptions evaluation raises beyond mbse-schemas' own (`NameError`, `ImportError`,
  `OverflowError`, `ZeroDivisionError`): Python's own in Python, and classes of the same names in TypeScript.

### Domains as data, in the framework

As built in mbse-expressions 0.9.0, by domains as data's stages 1 to 4 (PRs #27, #28, #30 and #31), in both languages;
the choices made where the decisions were silent are [the port's API](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/port/API.md),
Settled in domains as data's stages 1 to 4, items 112 to 161. The domains themselves are
[CAP-097: value domains of published standards](CAP-097.md#value-domains)'s, the ABIs
[CAP-267: C and C++ terms read under the ABI their holder names](CAP-267.md)'s and the standards
[CAP-268: standards as tagged data](CAP-268.md)'s.

- **The framework's install** adds, beside `Term`, `Expression` and `HasRoot`: `Expressions.Framework.Dialect`, the kind
  union of the dialects' singleton schemas; `DomainKind`, the union of the dialects' unions of kinds; `Domain`,
  `Defines` and `HasDomain`, with `Schemas.OfAny`'s extension adjacency `domain`; `Operates`; `HasAbi`, with
  `Expression`'s adjacency `abi`; and `Standard`, `Revision`, `Covers` and `HasStandard`, with `Schemas.OfObject`'s
  extension adjacency `standards`. Every dialect's singleton schema has the adjacencies `domains`, `operations` and
  `standards`.
- **Install order is the caller's** (Decided, "No `Uses`"): every caller (the suites, the tutorials' toolkit, the corpus
  and the skill) runs the installs in order, each its own transaction, the framework's, then the standard dialects',
  then the dialects that use them, and then the `define`s, which write the singletons' entries (an install cannot bind
  its own singleton): `Patterns.define`, the standard dialects', C's, C++'s, the ABI dialects', SystemVerilog's,
  Python3's, TypeScript5's, MATLAB's and Excel's. Nothing records or checks which dialects a dialect uses (API 116,
  118, 127).
- **Through the holder** (Decided, "No lone terms"): validation, inference and evaluation that read stored data reach
  the store of a term's holder, the `Expressions.Framework.Expression` holding its root, with no new argument:
  `Proxies.store_of(term)`, and `Terms.holder_of(value)`, the holder found by its owners. `Terms.Kind` has
  `operations(store)`: for a kind whose operations are data, those its dialect names in a store, by name. So a
  Patterns call is validated against the operations Patterns' singleton names (`Domains.operations`, each one's arity
  its pattern's variables, `Domains.arity`), in place of the code table `SIGNATURES`, which inference keeps: "'f' is
  not an operation Patterns defines", "add takes 2 arguments, got 1". A lone term that reads stored data is refused by
  mbse-schemas' `TypeError`, "the value does not belong to a store"; a term that reads none (another dialect's, or a
  SystemVerilog sized vector's) is read alone as before (API 125, 128, 140, 145).
- **The reading interfaces**: `Framework.Domains` reads a stored domain (`kind_of`, `properties`, `name_of`), a
  dialect's domains (`domains`) and its operations (`operations`, `arity`); `Framework.Standards` a dialect's editions
  ([CAP-268: standards as tagged data](CAP-268.md#as-built-mbse-expressions)) (API 117, 140, 155).
- **The package root exports `Framework` alone** (Decided, below): `mbse.Expressions` (`__all__ = ["Framework"]`) and
  `@mbse/expressions` re-export no dialect; each dialect, Patterns among them, is imported from its own path (`from
  mbse.Expressions.Patterns.Expressions import Evaluators, Expressions as E`), and Patterns is
  `Expressions.Patterns.Expressions`, a family with one dialect, not a part of the framework (API 121, 122).
- **What only Patterns' code domains used went**: `Same`'s `family`, and validation's refusal of an operator outside a
  closed kind's code vocabulary (API 130).

## Open questions

- **The framework and dialects as model, beyond the port's stage 2.** [PRI-018: no model needs code to read or write](../PRINCIPLES.md#PRI-018) ("No model should need code to
  read or write. Ever.") is applied by the port (Resolved, "Porting to the stabilized mbse-schemas"), and since 0.9.0
  to every dialect's domains, Patterns' operations and every dialect's standards (Domains as data, in the framework,
  above); what stays open is the other vocabularies as data: the other dialects' operations, inference's signatures
  ([CAP-232: domains inferred for terms](CAP-232.md)), `Callable`, a reference object, and `HasArguments`, relating a
  `Callable` to its parameters (the term kinds' starting point, Resolved), functions, formals and intrinsics, and value
  parameters with mbse-schemas' Parametrics, all after the port (its question 11). Until then a term names what it
  refers to, a callable or a domain, by name.

- `Symbolics` has imports, an expression's dependencies, but no exports. Exports would name what a unit of
  expressions (a module of constraints, a MATLAB package, a workbook's defined names) provides to others; they need a
  unit that groups expressions, which no dialect has yet.

## Decided

As the user decided, on mbse-expressions' domains as data: adopted, each the rule as stated, quoted as given.

- **C. No lone terms** (2026-11-06, on stage 1's design): "there is no valid programming context where a lone term needs
  a domain or schema". A term is validated and inferred within its holder, an `Expression` in a store with its
  dialect, which reaches the store (`Proxies.store_of`); nothing is designed for a term alone.
- **F. (a): the caller runs each install in order** (2026-11-06, approved "yes"): each its own transaction, as the
  tutorials' toolkit and the tests do; an install names the kinds it uses by their schemas' names, so mbse-schemas
  refuses it where they are not installed; ~~each install writes its uses as data, `Uses(dialect, used)`~~
  (*superseded* 2026-11-09 by "No `Uses`", below); no `install_all`. Every caller's list (tests, tutorials, toolkit,
  skill) installs the standard dialects before the dialects that use them.
- **G. Already decided** (2026-11-06): "I think this was already resolved and you may be leaking context". The
  namespace's rule, "`Expressions.<family>.<dialect>`, a family named as its standard names the language", and a family
  of one unversioned dialect naming it `Expressions` ([CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md#decided), "The
  namespace"), give `Expressions.IEEE754.Expressions` and the others, each family spelled as its standards body spells
  the standard. A standard dialect's shape (a singleton, no terms yet) stands as the agent's choice.
- **No `Uses`** (2026-11-09, I, on who writes `Uses`): "drop `Uses` and monitor for reasons to reinstate it in the
  future, stop and ask if you find one". Of the explicit install list every caller writes, `for module in (Framework,
  ISOIEC11404, IEEE754, IEEE1164, Unicode, Patterns, Python3, Matlab, Excel, Latex, C, Cpp, SystemVerilog):
  store.transaction(module.install)`, the user had said "I mean for now this looks pretty clean", and of `Uses`,
  "`Uses` is why we were discussing dependency notation". With the list kept, `Uses` has no job: **a dialect's
  dependencies are its caller's install order**; nothing read `Uses`. So `Expressions.Framework.Uses` and every
  dialect's adjacency `uses` are removed, in both languages, with their tests. This supersedes F (a)'s `Uses`, above.
  - **No check of install order** (2026-11-09). The first record of I called mbse-schemas' refusal of an install that
    names a schema not yet registered "the check" of install order; stage 1's review found it checks only an install
    that names another dialect's schemas (Python3's of IEEE 754's), not every dialect, though the skill said "an install
    whose dialects are missing is refused". Asked whether that was an overclaim or a reason to reinstate `Uses`, the
    user: "the whole thing seems a bit contrived if you ask me". So nothing claims to check install order: the order is
    the caller's list, the claim is removed from the records, the skill and the framework's docstrings, and no test is
    added for it; `Uses` stays dropped.
  - **Standing instruction**, the user's: "monitor for reasons to reinstate it in the future, stop and ask if you find
    one". An agent that finds a reason for `Uses`, something that needs to read as data which dialects a dialect uses,
    stops and asks the user before building it or anything in its place. Neither `HasAbi`
    ([CAP-267: C and C++ terms read under the ABI their holder names](CAP-267.md#decided), P) nor `Covers` and
    `HasStandard` ([CAP-268: standards as tagged data](CAP-268.md)) is one: each records something else, and nothing
    reads which dialects a dialect uses.
- **The package root exports `Framework` alone** (2026-11-09): to the proposal, asked in stage 1's pull request, that
  `mbse.Expressions` (and `@mbse/expressions`) export `Framework` alone, each dialect imported from its own path, the
  user: "approved". So the root's re-exports of Patterns' `Domains`, `Evaluators`, `Expressions` and `Partials` go,
  and its docstring's "the neutral form of every constraint" with them: they were one source of the habit of
  consolidating things under Patterns ([CAP-180: ISO/IEC 11404 datatypes replace the Basic expression dialect](CAP-180.md#decided), E).
  Patterns is imported from its own path, as every other dialect is. A breaking change, in 0.9.0.
- **Patterns' place: (b)** (2026-11-09, on stage 1's review). The user did not remember creating
  `Expressions.Framework.Patterns`: it came from the agent's recommendation in the namespace rename's batch ("Patterns,
  the neutral form and no standard's language"), approved with "yes, go". Shown the options, the user chose "(b)":
  `Expressions.Patterns.Expressions`, a family with one dialect named `Expressions`, as C's, MATLAB's and Excel's are.
  Renamed everywhere, in both languages, with no alias: its schemas, singleton, holder, install's and define's uids,
  Python package (`mbse/Expressions/Patterns/Expressions/`), TypeScript module (`src/Patterns/Expressions/`), the
  package's exports and every import, test, tutorial, the toolkit, the corpus, the skill, the README, llms.txt and
  EQUIVALENCE. "The neutral form" is retired: it describes Patterns nowhere. The branch tag of
  `Expressions.Framework.Term` stays `patterns`, as the tags do "for now". This supersedes the namespace's "Patterns is
  `Expressions.Framework.Patterns`" ([CAP-226: complete expression dialects](CAP-226.md#decided)).

## Decided by an agent, for review

Recorded in mbse-expressions' design, not marked as the user's: each *proposed* until reviewed.

- A dialect may extend another, inheriting its kinds; a base accepts its extensions' terms as arguments, and the
  extension validates and evaluates the trees that mix them, so that a dialect such as mbse-patterns 0.9.1's predicates adds
  proper terms (quantifiers over a store's objects, links) to Basic without copying it.
- A function may take a range of numbers of arguments: its signature takes any number (arity -1) and its kind checks
  the range, in validation and in evaluation alike. Excel's `IF` takes 2 or 3 (FALSE without an alternative), and
  `AND`, `OR`, `SUM`, `MIN` and `MAX` 1 to 255, as Excel's do.
- Expressions are partitioned into dialects over one framework (`Framework`), each declared by its kinds' roles;
  translators are pairwise, declared as bidirectional pattern rules, and applied by co-traversal.
- **(mbse-expressions port, API 11)** An import kind's names are read from its attributes, `Kind.names(attributes)` in
  place of `names(record)`, so that a translator can tell which names an import it writes binds before it is written
  (a `Prelude`).

Recorded in [the port's API, Settled in domains as data's stage 2](https://github.com/pitaman71/mbse-expressions/blob/main/docs/proposals/port/API.md#settled-in-domains-as-datas-stage-2-for-the-users-review),
items 124 to 130, not marked as the user's: each *proposed* until reviewed.

- **(mbse-expressions 28, domains as data, API 125)** **`Terms.Kind` gains `operations(store)`**: for a kind whose
  operations are data, those its dialect defines in a store, by name, each its `Schemas.OfExpression`; Patterns'
  `call` gives `Domains.operations` of its singleton found by its path, and keeps `vocabulary`, `SIGNATURES`, for
  `infer`.
- **(mbse-expressions 28, domains as data, API 125)** **A lone term is refused, with no fallback**: a term held in an
  `Expression`, however deep, reaches its holder's store, as its value objects are owned in turn; a lone term belongs to
  no store, and mbse-schemas' `TypeError` stands: no argument and no fallback is designed for it.
- **(mbse-expressions 28, domains as data, API 125)** **The data says so**: a store where `Patterns.define` has not run
  names no operation, and every call is refused so.
- **(mbse-expressions 28, domains as data, API 125)** **Only a kind that reads stored data reaches the store**: every
  other dialect's validation read its code vocabularies until stage 3, and validates a lone term that reads no stored
  data as before; SystemVerilog's evaluator, which validates a term's structure before evaluating it, is unchanged.
- **(mbse-expressions 28, domains as data, API 130)** **What no input reaches goes**: `Same`'s `family` and
  validation's refusal of an operator outside a closed kind's code vocabulary were used by Patterns alone
  ([PRI-039: 100% coverage of both](../PRINCIPLES.md#PRI-039)); `closed` stays, evaluation's.
- **(mbse-expressions 28, domains as data, stage 2's design)** **A dialect's operations come back by name**: read back
  from a snapshot they come in their names' order, as mbse-schemas writes a relation's entries sorted by its key
  ([PRI-001: declaration order](../PRINCIPLES.md#PRI-001)'s exception), so `Domains.operations` claims no order;
  validation reads them by name.
- **(mbse-expressions 30, domains as data, API 140)** **`Terms.holder_of(value)`** (`holderOf`), a term's holding
  `Expression` found by its owners, the term's record owned by its parent's, up to the reference object that holds the
  root; and **`Framework.Domains.domains(dialect)`**, a dialect's domains by name, which C's, C++'s and SystemVerilog's
  lookups read, found missing in building stage 3.
